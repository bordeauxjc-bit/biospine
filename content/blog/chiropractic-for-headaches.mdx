Not all headaches are the same, and that matters when it comes to treatment. A cluster headache responds to different care than a tension headache. A cervicogenic headache — one coming from the neck — responds very differently from a classic migraine.

The first step in treating a headache effectively is correctly identifying what kind it is.

## The three headache types we see most often

### Tension-type headaches

The most common kind. They feel like a band of pressure around the head, often worse at the end of the day. The pain is usually moderate, dull, and non-throbbing. Triggers include:

- Prolonged screen time
- Poor posture (forward-head position)
- Neck and shoulder muscle tension
- Stress and jaw clenching
- Dehydration

**Chiropractic care helps significantly** with tension-type headaches. We address the muscular and postural patterns that drive them.

### Cervicogenic headaches

These start in the neck and refer pain up into the head. They&rsquo;re often one-sided, aggravated by neck movement, and accompanied by reduced neck range of motion. If you&rsquo;ve ever woken up with a "crick" in your neck that turns into a headache, you&rsquo;ve probably experienced one.

**Chiropractic care is often the most effective treatment** for cervicogenic headaches because it directly addresses the source: the upper cervical spine.

### Migraines

Migraines are neurological events, not muscular ones. They typically involve:

- Throbbing pain, often one-sided
- Nausea
- Sensitivity to light or sound
- Sometimes visual "aura" before the headache starts

Chiropractic care can help **some** migraine patients — particularly those whose migraines are triggered or worsened by neck issues. But migraines that are clearly neurological in origin are often best managed with a neurologist or headache specialist alongside any chiropractic care.

## How chiropractic helps headaches

When chiropractic is appropriate for your headache type, treatment usually includes:

1. **Targeted upper-cervical adjustments** to restore motion in the neck joints that commonly refer pain into the head
2. **Soft-tissue work** on the suboccipital muscles (the small, tight muscles at the base of the skull)
3. **Postural correction** — because forward-head posture is a massive headache driver
4. **Trigger identification** — screens, sleep position, stress, jaw clenching
5. **Specific home exercises** to strengthen the deep neck flexors and stretch the tight muscles pulling your head forward

## What the research says

A well-designed chiropractic care plan has **strong evidence** for:

- Tension-type headaches (multiple randomized trials)
- Cervicogenic headaches (one of the most-studied applications of cervical manipulation)
- Reducing migraine frequency in some patients, especially those with a neck component

## What to expect from a course of care

- **Visit 1:** Thorough evaluation to identify what kind of headache we&rsquo;re dealing with, what&rsquo;s triggering it, and whether chiropractic is the right fit.
- **Early visits:** Focus on calming the acute tension and restoring motion.
- **Middle visits:** Address the underlying drivers — posture, ergonomics, stress patterns.
- **Later visits:** Move toward a maintenance rhythm or discharge you entirely.

If headaches are interfering with your work, sleep, or family time, you don&rsquo;t have to keep living with them.

## When to see a different kind of doctor

Not every headache belongs in a chiropractor&rsquo;s office. **Seek medical evaluation** if your headache:

- Is the "worst headache of your life" and came on suddenly
- Follows a head injury
- Is accompanied by fever, confusion, vision loss, or weakness
- Has changed significantly in character or pattern recently
- Wakes you up at night

These can indicate something that needs to be ruled out before we start conservative care.
