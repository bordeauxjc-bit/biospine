The urine check performed during a DOT physical is different from a DOT drug test. An employer may require both, which is why it helps to confirm exactly what has been ordered before booking.

If you are scheduling a [DOT physical at BioSpine in Lake City](/services/dot-physicals), tell the office whether you need a medical certification exam, an employer-ordered drug test, or have paperwork requesting both. Confirm that each required service has been arranged rather than assuming one appointment covers everything.

## What does the DOT physical urine test check?

The federal [Medical Examination Report, Form MCSA-5875](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/medical/medical-examination-report-form-commercial-driver-medical-certification), records urine specific gravity, protein, blood, and sugar. These findings help the Medical Examiner assess health issues that may need clarification.

An abnormal result does not provide a complete diagnosis on its own. Depending on the finding and your history, the examiner may ask for more information or recommend follow-up with the clinician managing your medical care. The [official examination form](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/2025-04/Medical%20Examination%20Report%20Form%20MCSA-5875%20508pdf.pdf) shows how this check fits into the broader exam.

This medical urinalysis does not serve as an employer's DOT drug screen.

## How is a DOT drug test different?

A DOT drug test is a separate testing process governed by federal drug-testing rules. For example, the Department of Transportation's [urine-testing requirements in 49 CFR Part 40](https://www.transportation.gov/odapc/part40/40-85) specify laboratory testing for particular drugs or metabolites, with initial and confirmation procedures.

A medical certificate and a drug-testing result answer different questions. Completing one does not establish that the other requirement has been completed. DOT's [rule on the relationship between medical and drug tests](https://www.transportation.gov/odapc/part40/40-13) explains the distinction, including when medical testing may use a remaining specimen after a DOT collection is completed.

## Three questions to ask before booking

1. **What exactly does my employer require?** Ask for the written order or instructions, including any designated collection location.
2. **Which services are confirmed at this appointment?** Verify the medical exam and any separate testing independently. Call the office to confirm where your employer's particular testing order can be completed.
3. **Which documents and deadlines apply?** Bring the employer's instructions and ask whether there is a specific authorization or reporting process to follow.

For drivers traveling from Scranton, Coward, Kingstree, or other Pee Dee communities, making those calls first can prevent a trip that only fulfills part of the employer's request.

## Bring a complete medication list

Tell the Medical Examiner about prescriptions, over-the-counter products, and ongoing treatment. Answer the health-history questions fully. Do not stop prescribed medication or leave it off the form because you are concerned about testing; ask the appropriate clinician how it should be documented.

The [DOT exam preparation checklist](/blog/what-to-bring-dot-physical) covers glasses, hearing aids, medication information, and condition-specific paperwork. Keep private medical details out of the website's scheduling message.
