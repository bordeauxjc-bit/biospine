Four out of five adults will experience meaningful low back pain at some point in their lives. For most, it resolves within a few weeks. For others, it lingers — and that&rsquo;s usually when chiropractic care becomes the right next step.

Here&rsquo;s how to think about low back pain, what you can try at home, and when to call a chiropractor.

## Common causes of low back pain

Most low back pain is **mechanical** — meaning it comes from the muscles, joints, ligaments, or discs of the spine. The most common culprits include:

- **Sprains and strains** from lifting, twisting, or overexertion
- **Disc-related pain** from bulging or herniated discs
- **Facet joint irritation** (the small joints where vertebrae meet)
- **Sciatica** — nerve irritation radiating down a leg
- **Postural pain** from prolonged sitting or standing
- **Deconditioning** — weakness in the muscles that support the spine

Mechanical back pain is, in most cases, a good candidate for chiropractic care.

## What you can try first

For mild, recent-onset low back pain without warning signs, try these for **48–72 hours** before seeking care:

1. **Keep moving gently.** Complete bed rest is one of the worst things you can do. Short walks are great.
2. **Use ice for the first 48 hours** (20 minutes on, 20 minutes off) to calm inflammation.
3. **After 48 hours, switch to heat** if it helps loosen tight muscles.
4. **Avoid the movement that caused it** for a few days — but don&rsquo;t avoid movement entirely.
5. **Sleep on your back or side**, with a pillow between or under your knees.

If you&rsquo;re not making progress within 3–5 days, or if the pain is getting worse, it&rsquo;s time to see someone.

## Signs you should call a chiropractor

Book an appointment if:

- The pain has lasted more than a week
- You&rsquo;ve had multiple episodes of the same kind of pain
- Pain is limiting work, sleep, or daily activities
- You&rsquo;re starting to compensate with poor movement patterns
- You feel stiff or "stuck" in certain positions
- The pain radiates into a buttock, hip, or leg

## Red flags — seek urgent care instead

A small number of symptoms indicate that low back pain may be caused by something more serious. **Go to an emergency room or urgent care** — not a chiropractor — if you have any of these:

- Loss of bladder or bowel control
- Numbness in the groin or inner thighs ("saddle anesthesia")
- Significant, progressive weakness in a leg or foot
- Pain following major trauma (a fall, a car accident)
- Fever, chills, or unexplained weight loss alongside the pain
- History of cancer with new-onset back pain

These are rare, but they&rsquo;re worth knowing.

## What a chiropractor can do

When your pain is mechanical and conservative care is appropriate, chiropractic has strong research backing for low back pain. A typical care plan involves:

- A thorough exam to identify the source of the pain
- **Spinal adjustments** to restore motion to stiff joints
- **Soft-tissue work** to release guarded muscles
- **Specific home exercises** to build the support muscles your spine needs
- Guidance on ergonomics, sleeping positions, and daily movements

Most patients with mechanical low back pain see meaningful improvement within **2–6 visits** — often much sooner.

## What to expect timeline-wise

Every patient is different, but here&rsquo;s a reasonable expectation for straightforward mechanical low back pain:

- **Visit 1:** Evaluation + first adjustment. Some patients feel relief immediately.
- **Visits 2–4:** Progressive improvement in pain and function.
- **Visits 5–6:** Most patients are near baseline or have a clear trajectory.
- **Beyond 6 visits:** If you&rsquo;re not making expected progress, we reassess — no one should stay in care without measurable results.
