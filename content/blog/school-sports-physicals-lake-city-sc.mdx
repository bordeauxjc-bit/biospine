“School physical” can refer to several different things: a preparticipation sports examination, an annual well-child visit, a camp form, or the immunization certificate used for school attendance. Lake City families can save time by asking the school exactly which document is due before booking an appointment.

## Can a South Carolina chiropractor perform a school physical?

[South Carolina's chiropractic law](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c009.php) defines chiropractic analysis to include a physical examination. The [South Carolina Board of Chiropractic Examiners](https://www.llr.sc.gov/chiro/scope.aspx) also states that licensed chiropractors may perform procedures normally used in chiropractic physical examinations, within the profession's scope.

Whether that examination satisfies a particular school's paperwork is a separate question. The current [South Carolina High School League physical form](https://schsl.org/archives/2353) includes an athlete history and preparticipation examination, and its medical-eligibility signature line identifies **MD, DO, NP, or PA**. Schools and athletic programs may require their exact form and may limit which credentials they accept.

Before booking, send or bring the current form and ask both the school and BioSpine whether a South Carolina D.C. may complete it. Do not rely on an old form or assume every “school physical” has the same signer rule.

Florence School District 3 has also announced sports physicals and well-child services through the [HopeHealth school-based clinic at Lake City High School](https://www.hope-health.org/locations-list/hopehealth-lake-city-high-lake-city-sc/). Availability and eligibility can change, so contact that clinic or the school directly before going.

## School attendance uses an immunization certificate

South Carolina requires students attending childcare and K-12 schools to have the appropriate South Carolina Certificate of Immunization or a valid exemption. That certificate is separate from the SCHSL sports physical.

The [South Carolina Department of Public Health school-vaccine page](https://dph.sc.gov/health-wellness/child-teen-health/vaccine-requirements-info) publishes the current school-year requirements. DPH also provides access to eligible records through the [SIMON public portal](https://dph.sc.gov/public/vaccinations/simon-portal-faqs).

BioSpine does not administer vaccines. Contact the child's primary-care practice, a participating health department, or an appropriate school-based clinic for immunization questions and certificates.

## What to bring to a sports physical

The examining practice may ask for:

- The school's current physical form with the parent and athlete sections completed
- A list of medications and allergies
- Glasses, contact lenses, inhalers, or other equipment used during activity
- Details about prior injuries, surgery, fainting, chest symptoms, concussions, or activity restrictions
- Relevant records from cardiology, orthopedics, neurology, or another treating clinician
- The parent or guardian required to give consent

Do not omit a previous symptom or injury simply to finish the form. The purpose of the history is to identify issues that need evaluation before strenuous activity.

## When the concern is an injury instead of a form

A preparticipation physical answers whether the student is medically eligible for sports under the school form. It is not designed to diagnose every painful knee, ankle, shoulder, or back complaint.

BioSpine may evaluate selected musculoskeletal injuries and movement-related pain after first screening for fractures, concussions, major tears, unstable injuries, and other problems that need a different provider. Read [sports injury care: what BioSpine treats and refers](/blog/sports-injury-chiropractic) for that distinction.

Suspected concussion, fainting during activity, chest pain, trouble breathing, a deformed joint, inability to bear weight, or rapidly worsening neurological symptoms require prompt medical evaluation—not a routine chiropractic visit.
