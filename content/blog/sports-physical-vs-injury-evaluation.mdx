A sports physical and a sports-injury evaluation answer different questions. Booking the wrong visit can leave a student without the school form they need or delay care for an injury that needs attention.

## Choose a sports physical for participation clearance

A preparticipation sports physical reviews the athlete's medical history and performs a general examination to decide whether the student is medically eligible for school sports. South Carolina chiropractors may perform physical examinations within chiropractic scope, but the current South Carolina High School League form identifies an MD, DO, NP, or PA on its medical-eligibility signature line.

Ask the school which form and credentials it accepts, then call BioSpine with that exact form so the office can confirm whether Dr. Jordan can complete it. See the [Lake City school and sports physical guide](/blog/school-sports-physicals-lake-city-sc) for the official form, South Carolina immunization information, and a local school-based clinic resource.

## Choose an injury evaluation for pain or lost function

An injury evaluation focuses on what happened, which movements or tissues appear involved, whether imaging or referral is needed, and what the athlete must be able to do before returning.

BioSpine may be an appropriate starting point for selected joint, muscle, or movement-related complaints such as:

- Back or neck pain after lifting, training, or contact
- Joint stiffness or restricted movement without signs of an unstable injury
- Selected overuse complaints that developed as training volume changed
- Pain that has not settled after an earlier medical evaluation

Dr. Jordan examines the problem before deciding whether chiropractic care, another conservative treatment, imaging, rehabilitation, or referral makes sense.

## Choose urgent medical care for warning signs

Do not wait for a routine office appointment after:

- Suspected concussion, especially confusion, vomiting, worsening headache, balance trouble, or unusual behavior
- Chest pain, fainting, or severe shortness of breath during activity
- A visibly deformed joint or suspected fracture
- Inability to bear weight after an acute injury
- New weakness, spreading numbness, or loss of bladder or bowel control
- Severe pain with fever or an open wound

Call emergency services when symptoms are severe or life-threatening. School concussion and return-to-play procedures still apply even when the athlete feels better quickly.

## Clearance is not the same as return to play

A preseason physical completed before an injury does not clear an athlete to return after a later concussion, fracture, dislocation, or other significant problem. Return decisions should be based on the new diagnosis, function, sport demands, and the instructions of the clinician managing the injury.

Likewise, reduced pain alone does not prove that an athlete is ready for sprinting, cutting, jumping, throwing, or contact.

## What to tell the office

When requesting an injury appointment, explain:

- How and when it happened
- The exact location of pain or other symptoms
- Whether the athlete could continue playing
- Any swelling, bruising, numbness, weakness, dizziness, or headache
- Prior imaging, diagnoses, treatment, and school restrictions
- The sport and activities the student needs to resume

This helps the office decide whether BioSpine is a reasonable first stop or whether a medical, orthopedic, or emergency evaluation should come first.
