Walking into a chiropractor's office for the first time can feel like stepping into the unknown. What will they do? Will it hurt? How long will it take? These are fair questions, and every new patient deserves honest answers before they book.

Here’s exactly what to expect on your first visit to our Lake City office.

## Before you arrive

Plan to arrive about **10 minutes early** so you have time to complete new-patient paperwork without feeling rushed. Bring:

- A valid photo ID
- Your insurance card, if you’re using insurance
- Any recent imaging (X-rays, MRIs, or CT scans from the past year)
- A list of current medications and supplements
- A list of previous surgeries, major injuries, or chronic diagnoses

Wear comfortable clothes that allow you to move, stretchy athletic wear works great. If the exam requires a gown, we’ll provide one.

## Step 1. The consultation

Once you’re settled in the exam room, we’ll sit down and talk. Not at you, **with** you. I want to understand what brought you in, when the pain started, what makes it better or worse, and what you’re hoping to get back to doing. This conversation usually takes 10–15 minutes and it’s the single most important part of your visit.

Good diagnoses start with good history-taking. I’ll ask questions like:

- When did you first notice the pain?
- Is it sharp, dull, burning, or radiating?
- Does anything make it better or worse?
- Have you had this type of pain before?
- What’s your day-to-day work and activity level?

## Step 2. The physical exam

Next comes a hands-on exam. I’ll check your posture, range of motion, and how your spine and joints are moving. I’ll also run targeted **orthopedic and neurological tests**, simple motions and pressure tests, to help pinpoint exactly what’s going on.

The exam is thorough but not painful. If anything I do causes sharp pain, tell me and I’ll stop.

## Step 3. Imaging (only if needed)

Most patients do **not** need X-rays or imaging on day one. I only recommend imaging when it will genuinely change your treatment plan, for example, after a significant fall, in the presence of certain red-flag symptoms, or when conservative care hasn’t produced expected progress.

I’ll never recommend a scan just to "have one on file."

## Step 4. Your diagnosis and care plan

After the exam, I’ll explain what I found in plain language. You’ll know:

- What I think is going on (the diagnosis)
- What’s causing it (the mechanism)
- What we can do about it (the plan)
- How many visits we’re likely talking about
- What results you should expect, and by when

If chiropractic isn’t the right fit for what you’re experiencing, I’ll tell you that, too, and help you find the right kind of provider.

## Step 5. Your first adjustment

If chiropractic care is appropriate, we’ll usually do your first adjustment the same day. The adjustment itself is quick, seconds, not minutes, and most patients describe immediate relief of pressure or stiffness. You may hear a popping sound; that’s simply gas releasing from the joint, just like cracking your knuckles. It’s not a bone moving out of place.

After the adjustment, I’ll give you specific guidance for the next 24–48 hours: ice or heat, movements to avoid, and gentle exercises to reinforce what we just did.

## How long will it take?

Plan for **45 to 60 minutes** for your first visit. Follow-up visits are typically **15 to 30 minutes**.

## Will it hurt?

Chiropractic adjustments are usually not painful. Some patients feel mild soreness afterward, similar to the soreness you’d feel after a good workout, which typically resolves within 24 hours. If your condition is acutely inflamed, we’ll often start gentler and build up as tolerated.
